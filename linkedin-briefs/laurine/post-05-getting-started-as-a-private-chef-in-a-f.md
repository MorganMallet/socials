Title: Getting started as a private chef in a family home
1. Restaurant and five-star hotel experience counts, and Michelin training stands out.
2. Be ready to cook for a crowd, because a summer villa can host 20 to 70 guests.
3. Learn to work fast under pressure, and help the butler for an hour when the house is busy.
4. Some families ask specifically for a chef who is humble and flexible.
5. In the US, private chefs earn $100,000 to $300,000 a year.
Footer: Laurine Mallet, co-founder, Morgan & Mallet

CAPTION
Private chefs are among the roles families ask us for most. In the US they're the third most requested role, and in the UAE the second.

In St-Tropez we had a client who received between 20 and 70 people at his villa. The chef we had placed with him for the rest of the year traveled down to St-Tropez, and we recruited a commis to help, because nobody can cook for 70 people alone.

It's a different pace from a restaurant. The family may ask you for something without sitting down to explain, and on a busy day you might help the butler for an hour.

One family in Switzerland asked me for a chef who was humble and flexible. It took a lot of interviews to find him, and seven years later he still works for them.
